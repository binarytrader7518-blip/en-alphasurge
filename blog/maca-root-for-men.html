Maca Root for Men: Tradition, Studies and Realistic Expectations

By the Alpha Surge Editorial Team · Updated 25 November 2026

Maca (Lepidium meyenii) is a radish-like root grown high in the Peruvian Andes. Locals have eaten it as food for centuries, and it has a long traditional reputation for stamina and vitality. It’s one of five ingredients in Alpha Surge.

The Key Study in Men

In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial published in Andrologia, 57 healthy men aged 21–56 took maca or placebo for 12 weeks. Men in the maca groups reported improved sexual desire from week 8 onward. Notably, the researchers found this was not explained by changes in mood, anxiety or blood testosterone.

What Government Sources Say

MedlinePlus notes that reliable evidence for most of maca’s claimed benefits is still limited, and that it appears low-risk in food amounts for most people. That’s a fair summary: promising, but not proven.

Realistic Expectations

  • Studies used specific maca preparations and doses.
  • Effects reported were subjective and appeared after weeks, not days.
  • Maca does not appear to work by raising testosterone.
