Ashwagandha and Everyday Stress: A Plain-English Guide

By the Alpha Surge Editorial Team · Updated 25 November 2026

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) has been used in Ayurvedic practice for centuries. Its name refers to the smell of its root, and it’s sometimes called Indian ginseng or winter cherry. It’s the stress-balance ingredient in Alpha Surge.

What the Research Suggests

The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements reviews clinical trials in which adults taking ashwagandha extracts for six to eight weeks reported less perceived stress, and some studies found lower cortisol levels. A meta-analysis also found a small but significant improvement in sleep. However, studies were small, mostly conducted in India, and used many different preparations.

Safety First

NCCIH notes ashwagandha may be safe for short-term use, but long-term safety hasn’t been established. Possible side effects include drowsiness and stomach upset. It’s not recommended for people with certain thyroid or autoimmune conditions or hormone-sensitive prostate cancer.

Why Stress Matters for Men’s Vitality

High stress disrupts sleep, saps motivation and makes training feel harder. Lowering stress is often the quickest way to feel more like yourself.

Stress Habits That Pair Well

  • A short walk after lunch.
  • Five minutes of slow breathing before bed.
  • A consistent wake-up time, even on weekends.
